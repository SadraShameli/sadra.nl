## Overview

Lightning Funded is Tradeify's instant-funded product: traders purchase direct access to a Sim Funded account and skip the evaluation phase entirely — confirmed both by "Lightning Funded Accounts" ("Lightning Funded skips evaluation phases entirely, direct access to simulated funded account") and by the homepage's structured pricing data (every tier's `evaluation` array is empty). Lightning is offered in four account sizes (25K/50K/100K/150K); this file documents the $50,000 tier only, matching the size this repo's simulator engine currently models.

**Inactivity rule, now directly confirmed, resolving a previously-flagged engine/doc disagreement.** This tree's own simulator engine had already modeled a 7-day idle-closure rule for Lightning, citing a "Guidelines for Traders" article that, at the time, hadn't been located or read as a source for this file. That article has now been read directly (found via a full sitemap sweep of `help.tradeify.co`): "To keep your funded or evaluation account active, you must place at least one trade per week (Monday through Friday). This is per account, not per user." Stated without a per-plan qualifier, so treated as firm-wide, independently corroborated for every plan in `README.md`'s Firm-Wide Rules. "Essential Trading Rules Overview" independently corroborates the same weekly requirement and adds a detail neither "Guidelines for Traders" nor the Funded Trader Agreement states: the qualifying trade "can be as brief as 5 seconds as long as it satisfies the minimum activity requirement," so any trade counts, not just a substantively held one.

Once funded, Lightning Funded carries an End-of-Day trailing drawdown with the same lock mechanic used across Tradeify's other Sim Funded products, a Daily Loss Limit that escalates once the account crosses a 6%-profit threshold, and a progressive consistency requirement across payout requests (20% / 25% / 30%). Per Lightning's own dedicated payout-policy article, that consistency ladder applies only to accounts purchased after September 12, 2025 8:00 AM EST — accounts purchased before that cutoff remain on a flat 20% consistency rule for every payout. Lightning has no reset option at any stage: a failed account must be repurchased outright, not reset.

## How the Drawdown Works

Lightning Funded enforces an End-of-Day trailing max drawdown, applied identically to every Tradeify Sim Funded product per "Rules: Trailing Max Drawdowns": the drawdown limit trails the account's highest-ever EOD balance (its high-water mark) and only ever moves up, never down — a lower EOD balance than the prior day leaves the limit exactly where it is. Although the limit itself only _updates_ once per day at EOD, it is _enforced_ continuously: if net liquidation value touches or crosses the limit at any moment during the trading day, the account fails immediately and permanently, even if the balance recovers before that day's close ("a hard breach"... "different from the Daily Loss Limit (soft breach)").

On Sim Funded accounts specifically (not evaluations — irrelevant to Lightning, which has none), this trailing behavior eventually locks. Per "Rules: Trailing Max Drawdowns": the drawdown "locks once profit exceeds drawdown amount by $100, OR immediately when payout requested — whichever first." For the $50,000 tier, that trigger point is an EOD balance of $52,100 (the article's own per-plan lock table lists $52,100 for Lightning at 50K). Once locked, "After locking, the drawdown limit becomes a fixed floor that never moves up again." — for the $50,000 tier that fixed floor is $50,100 ("$100 above starting balance"). This is a separate mechanic from the Daily Loss Limit's own 6%-profit escalation (trigger: EOD balance $53,000, described in the Sim Funded table above) — the two thresholds are close in size ($52,100 vs. $53,000) but govern different things and must not be treated as the same event.

### Worked Example

This example uses the full nominal-balance convention: the $50,000 account size is the literal starting balance of the Sim Funded account (Lightning has no separate evaluation stage to convert from), and every step below tracks the account's actual EOD balance, not a separate $0-based profit counter.

1. Account opens funded at $50,000. Initial drawdown floor: $50,000 − $2,000 = $48,000. Initial Daily Loss Limit: $1,250.
2. Day 12 EOD: balance closes at $51,400, a new peak (profit $1,400). The floor trails up to $51,400 − $2,000 = $49,400. Profit has not yet crossed the $2,100 lock-trigger threshold, so the drawdown is still trailing normally, not locked.
3. Day 18 EOD: balance closes at $52,100, a new peak (profit exactly $2,100 — the exact lock-trigger balance from "Rules: Trailing Max Drawdowns"' own table). The drawdown now locks: its floor becomes permanently fixed at $50,100 ("$100 above starting balance") and will never move again, regardless of future EOD highs.
4. Day 25 EOD: balance closes at $54,500, a new peak (profit $4,500, past the $53,000 / 6%-profit DLL-escalation point reached on an earlier day). Without the lock, ordinary trailing math would move the floor to $54,500 − $2,000 = $52,500 — but because the account already locked on Day 18, the floor stays fixed at $50,100. Separately, because balance crossed $53,000 on an earlier day, the Daily Loss Limit is now $2,000 (up from $1,250) as of the next session after that crossing — a distinct, already-resolved event, not something Day 25 itself triggers.
5. Day 31: balance closes at $49,900 — below the locked $50,100 floor. This breaches the (now-fixed) drawdown and the account closes at EOD, regardless of how far above the floor it had climbed on Day 25.

Re-deriving from the first sentence: the account never left nominal-dollar-balance terms in any step (no step switched to a $0-based profit figure), and the final breach ($49,900 < locked floor $50,100) is consistent with a floor that stopped moving on Day 18 and never moved again — confirming the example is internally consistent under the stated convention.

**Payout Profit Goal — a separate concept from the request amount above.** "Lightning Funded: Account Payout Policy" is explicit that this is a distinct figure: "Your Profit Goal... is the profit you must earn to unlock a payout request. It is not the amount you can withdraw." For the $50,000 tier, purchased after September 12, 2025: Payout 1 requires $3,000 fresh profit (EOD balance $53,000) to unlock a request; Payout 2 onward requires $2,000 fresh profit per cycle, resetting after each payout. For accounts purchased before that cutoff (legacy), the article's separate table shows the same $3,000 Payout-1 goal but a $2,000 Payout-2 goal and $2,000 Payout-3-and-beyond goal (vs. $2,000/$2,000 current). Reaching the goal does not itself raise the withdrawable amount above the Max Payout per Cycle cap in the table above — e.g. exceeding the $3,000 Payout-1 goal does not let a $50K trader withdraw more than the $2,000 Payout-1 cap; the surplus remains in the account.

**Legacy accounts (purchased before September 12, 2025 8:00 AM EST).** Several rules differ from the current ones documented above, per "Lightning Funded Accounts" and "Lightning Funded: Account Payout Policy": Max Contracts at 50K is 5 minis / 50 micros (vs. 4/40 current); the Consistency Rule is a flat 20% for every payout (vs. the 20/25/30% ladder); the Daily Loss Limit is removed entirely at the 6%-profit threshold rather than increased to $2,000. The Payout Profit Goal table itself is unchanged at the 50K tier specifically — both the current and legacy tables give $3,000 (Payout 1) / $2,000 (Payout 2) / $2,000 (Payout 3+), identical figures; the two tables only actually diverge at the 100K and 150K tiers, which this file does not document. "Accounts purchased before new dashboard launch retain original parameters, including on reset" — though Lightning has no reset option regardless (see "Not Confirmed").

**The consistency threshold is inclusive, not strict.** "Any percentage at or below your limit meets the rule - 19.97% meets a 20% rule, and exactly 20.00% meets it too. Only a figure strictly above your limit fails."

## Live Transition

Read together with `elite-live.md`'s own confirmed source, these two framings are not actually in tension: "Lightning Funded: Account Payout Policy" states Tradeify's discretionary right — "Tradeify reserves the right to move you to a Live Funded Account at any time after a successful payout. Tradeify will contact you via email regarding this decision." — while separate homepage prose states a trader-facing bar: "Hit 3 approved payouts to qualify for Tradeify Elite." The Elite Program article (`elite-live.md`'s primary source) resolves the apparent gap directly: its stated eligibility thresholds are explicitly "minimum requirements for consideration, not automatic qualification," and Tradeify "evaluates traders holistically... most traders will not be transitioned to live immediately upon hitting these thresholds." Read this way, "any time after a successful payout" describes the outer bound of Tradeify's own discretion (it could in principle act as early as payout #1), while "3 payouts to qualify" is the stated minimum threshold before a trader is even considered — two different things, not two competing numbers for the same rule. Moderate-high confidence: this reconciliation is inferred from combining two of Tradeify's own already-confirmed statements, not a single source stating the resolution explicitly.

Elite Live's own operating parameters (starting balance, drawdown, DLL, contract limits, payout split, eligibility mechanics) are documented in full in the shared [`elite-live.md`](elite-live.md) file, not duplicated here.
