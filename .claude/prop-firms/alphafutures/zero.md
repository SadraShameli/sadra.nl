## Overview

Zero is Alpha Futures' entry evaluation product: a permanent $0 activation fee, no consistency requirement on the Evaluation (a trader may pass in as few as 1 trading day), and, per the help center's own description, a flat 90% profit split from the first eligible request with no tiered escalation (the signed General Service Agreement instead ties this to a tiered virtual-payout schedule; see the Sim Funded table's Profit Split row). It is offered in 25K, 50K, and 100K sizes; this file documents the 50K tier only, matching the size currently modeled in this repo's simulator engine (`src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/alphafutures/AlphaFutures.ts`).

On the Qualified (Sim Funded) side, traders may request performance fees up to 4 times per month after every 5 winning trading days of $200 or more, subject to a 40% consistency rule between requests. Alpha Futures markets Zero as not sliding the Maximum Loss Limit down toward the starting balance at a trader's first withdrawal, unlike some competing firms.

## How the Drawdown Works

The Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is an End of Day (EOD) trailing drawdown on every Alpha Futures account, calculated from the account's EOD balance high, not intraday equity high. It rises on each new EOD balance high and never moves down on a losing day. A breach at any point, "floating equity or closed balance of account," liquidates the account immediately, not only at end of day: on an Evaluation Account this means the account can no longer become Qualified, and on a Qualified Account it means the account is closed. Once the MLL reaches the account's own starting balance it stops trailing entirely and locks there for the life of the account (see Drawdown Lock row above).

The Daily Loss Guard ($1,000 at 50K, 2% of starting balance, a soft breach) is a separate, independent mechanic: if open or closed P&L reaches -2% of starting balance during the trading day, all open positions are flattened, pending orders are canceled, and the account is locked out of new trades until the next trading day begins (6 PM ET). It does not fail the account, and it resets every trading day.

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (e.g. a $50,000 account showing $500 profit reads as a $50,500 balance), not a $0-based profit figure, and never switches to a $0-based convention. Steps 1–5 (Evaluation) reproduce the Maximum Loss Limit article's own literal $50K worked figures; steps 6–10 (Sim Funded) are this file's own illustrative figures, built under the confirmed 5-winning-day/$200+ payout mechanic.

**Evaluation:**

1. Trader opens a $50,000 Zero 50K Evaluation. Starting MLL is $48,000 ($50,000 − $2,000).
2. EOD Day 1: the account makes $500, balance is $50,500 (a new EOD high). MLL trails up to $48,500 ($2,000 below the new high).
3. EOD Day 2: the account loses $500, balance returns to $50,000 (not a new high). MLL does not move down; it stays at $48,500.
4. EOD Day 3: balance grows to $52,000 (a new EOD high, $2,000 profit, equal to the $2,000 Drawdown Amount). This is the exact drawdown-lock trigger: the MLL stops trailing and locks permanently at $50,000, the account's own starting balance.
5. EOD Day 4: balance grows to $53,000 ($3,000 profit, the profit target). The Zero Evaluation is passed immediately: the 1-day minimum was already met on Day 1, and there is no consistency rule to satisfy on a Zero Evaluation.

**Sim Funded:** sources read for this file do not state whether the Sim Funded (Qualified) account's opening balance carries forward the Evaluation's ending balance ($53,000 in this example) or resets to the nominal $50,000 starting balance (see Not Confirmed). This example assumes a fresh $50,000 reset so the drawdown-trailing and no-slide-on-payout mechanics can be shown from their own starting points.

6. Trader activates the Zero 50K Sim Funded account at a fresh $50,000 balance. MLL starts at $48,000 again.
7. Five consecutive winning trading days, each $250 profit (each ≥ the $200 winning-day threshold): balance rises $50,250 → $50,500 → $50,750 → $51,000 → $51,250, a new EOD high each day, so the MLL trails up in step with it: $48,250 → $48,500 → $48,750 → $49,000 → $49,250. Total profit after 5 days: $1,250.
8. Five winning days of $200+ are now accumulated, so a payout request is eligible. Consistency check: the largest single day ($250) divided by net profit since the last request ($1,250) is 20%, under the 40% consistency threshold, so the request is not blocked.
9. Trader requests $600 (within the $200 minimum / $1,500 maximum per this file's Payouts table, and within the "up to 50% of profit" cap: 50% of $1,250 is $625, and $600 ≤ $625). Because this is the account's first virtual payout, the trader receives 70% of the requested amount, $420, as cash, under the signed General Service Agreement's tiered schedule (see the Profit Split row above). The $600 gross request is removed from the account balance: $51,250 − $600 = $50,650.
10. Per Zero's own no-slide rule, the MLL is unaffected by the withdrawal and remains at $49,250 (it does not move toward the new, lower balance). Final balance $50,650 re-derives from the first figure: $50,000 + ($250 × 5) − $600 = $50,650.

The user separately ruled, on 2026-09-24, that a subscription re-buy is a new account for this tree's billing model (T10); that decision was about billing continuity, not this payout-tier count, but it is consistent with treating a re-bought account as a fresh account here too. What remains genuinely unconfirmed is whether Alpha Futures counts virtual payouts per physical account, as the Agreement's wording implies, or per trader across every account they have ever purchased; no source addresses that distinction directly. Do not assume Alpha Futures' support team would confirm a per-account count if asked directly, or that the Agreement's "on the account" wording has necessarily been implemented in Alpha Futures' own systems the way this tree reads it.
