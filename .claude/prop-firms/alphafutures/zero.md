## Overview

Zero is Alpha Futures' entry evaluation product: a permanent $0 activation fee, no consistency requirement on the Evaluation (a trader may pass in as few as 1 trading day), and, per the help center's own description, a flat 90% profit split from the first eligible request with no tiered escalation (the signed General Service Agreement instead ties this to a tiered virtual-payout schedule; see the Sim Funded table's Profit Split row). It is offered in 25K, 50K, and 100K sizes; this file documents the 50K tier only, matching the size currently modeled in this repo's simulator engine (`src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/alphafutures/AlphaFutures.ts`).

On the Qualified (Sim Funded) side, traders may request performance fees up to 4 times per month after every 5 winning trading days of $200 or more, subject to a 40% consistency rule between requests. The Zero Account Overview states there is no "MLL slides to $0 at first withdrawal" rule ("If MLL was at $49,200 before payout, it will be after as well").

## How the Drawdown Works

The Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is an End of Day (EOD) trailing drawdown on every Alpha Futures account, calculated from the account's EOD balance high, not intraday equity high. It rises on each new EOD balance high and never moves down on a losing day. A breach at any point, "floating equity or closed balance of account," liquidates the account immediately, not only at end of day: on an Evaluation Account this means the account can no longer become Qualified, and on a Qualified Account it means the account is closed. Once the MLL reaches the account's own starting balance it stops trailing entirely and locks there for the life of the account (see Drawdown Lock row above).

The Daily Loss Guard ($1,000 at 50K; the article states "2% of starting account balance on Zero Accounts") is a soft-breach rule, separate from the MLL: if open or closed P&L reaches -2% during the trading day, all open positions are flattened, pending orders are canceled, and the account is prevented from placing any new trades until the start of the next trading day (6 PM ET). It does not fail the account ("you do not lose your Account").

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (e.g. a $50,000 account showing $500 profit reads as a $50,500 balance), not a $0-based profit figure, and never switches to a $0-based convention. The $50,000 opening balance is read from the "50K" tier label (Starting Balance is Unconfirmed). Steps 1–4 reproduce the Maximum Loss Limit article's own worked figures for a $50k Zero Account; steps 5–10 are this file's own illustrative figures, built on the 5-winning-day/$200+ payout mechanic, and each step that rests on an assumption or a derived figure says so.

**Evaluation:**

1. Trader opens a Zero 50K Evaluation at a $50,000 balance (read from the tier label). Starting MLL is $48,000 (derived: $50,000 − $2,000).
2. EOD Day 1: the account makes $500, balance is $50,500 (a new EOD high). MLL trails up to $48,500 ($2,000 below the new high).
3. EOD Day 2: the account loses $500, balance returns to $50,000 (not a new high). MLL does not move down; it stays at $48,500.
4. EOD Day 3: balance grows to $52,000 (a new EOD high, $2,000 profit, equal to the $2,000 Drawdown Amount). This is the exact drawdown-lock trigger: the MLL stops trailing and locks permanently at $50,000, the account's own starting balance.
5. EOD Day 4 (illustrative, not in the article): balance grows to $53,000 ($3,000 profit, the profit target). The Zero Evaluation is passed immediately: the 1-day minimum was already met on Day 1, and there is no consistency rule to satisfy on a Zero Evaluation.

**Sim Funded:** sources read for this file do not state whether the Sim Funded (Qualified) account's opening balance carries forward the Evaluation's ending balance ($53,000 in this example) or resets to the nominal $50,000 starting balance (see Not Confirmed). This example assumes a fresh $50,000 reset so the drawdown-trailing and no-slide-on-payout mechanics can be shown from their own starting points.

6. Trader activates the Zero 50K Sim Funded account at a fresh $50,000 balance (assumed, see above and Not Confirmed). MLL starts at $48,000 again (derived: $50,000 − $2,000 under that assumption).
7. Five consecutive winning trading days, each $250 profit (illustrative figures, each ≥ the $200 winning-day threshold): balance rises $50,250 → $50,500 → $50,750 → $51,000 → $51,250, a new EOD high each day, so the MLL trails up in step with it: $48,250 → $48,500 → $48,750 → $49,000 → $49,250 (derived: each EOD high minus $2,000). Total profit after 5 days: $1,250 (derived).
8. Five winning days of $200+ are now accumulated, so a payout request is eligible. Consistency check: the largest single day ($250) divided by net profit since the last request ($1,250) is 20% (derived), under the 40% consistency threshold, so the request is not blocked (a 20% day is below either reading of the boundary, which is Unconfirmed at exactly 40%).
9. Trader requests $600 (within the $200 minimum / $1,500 maximum per this file's Payouts table, and within the "up to 50% of profit" cap as the Payout Policy states it: 50% of $1,250 is $625 (derived), and $600 ≤ $625; the cap itself is Unconfirmed, see Not Confirmed). Assuming, for illustration only, the signed General Service Agreement's tiered schedule (the Profit Split is Unconfirmed, see the Profit Split row above), a first virtual payout would pay the trader 70% of the requested amount, $420 (derived), as cash; a flat 90% would pay $540 (derived). The $600 gross request is removed from the account balance: $51,250 − $600 = $50,650 (derived).
10. Per the Zero Account Overview's no-slide statement ("If MLL was at $49,200 before payout, it will be after as well"), the MLL is unaffected by the withdrawal and remains at $49,250 (it does not move toward the new, lower balance). Final balance $50,650 (derived) re-derives from the first figure: $50,000 + ($250 × 5) − $600 = $50,650.
