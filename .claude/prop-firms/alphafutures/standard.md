## Overview

Alpha Futures Standard is the firm's classic Evaluation to Qualified Account path, distinct from the Zero plan, the higher-cap Advanced plan, and the no-evaluation Direct plan. This file covers the $50,000 size only, the size the calculator engine models; Alpha Futures also sells $100,000 and $150,000 Standard accounts with their own targets and fees, not covered here (see Not Confirmed). Standard relaunched as a monthly-subscription product: there is no one-time evaluation fee, the Evaluation stage rebills every month until the trader passes or cancels, and the Qualified stage that follows carries no monthly fee of its own. Standard's own distinguishing features, per its dedicated overview article, are double the Zero plan's withdrawal caps, no Daily Loss Guard during the Evaluation, no activation fee, a marketed flat 90% performance split from the first eligible request with no tiered system (the signed General Service Agreement instead ties this to a tiered virtual-payout schedule; see the Sim Funded table's Profit Split row), and a rule that the Maximum Loss Limit does not slide toward the starting balance at the first payout.

The Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is Alpha Futures' name for its End-of-Day trailing drawdown, applied identically across every account at the firm, Evaluation and Qualified, per the firm-wide MLL article. It trails the account's EOD balance high, never trails down on a losing day, and permanently stops trailing (locks) once it would reach the account's own starting balance. Standard layers a 50% consistency rule on the Evaluation, dropping to 40% once Qualified, and a Daily Loss Guard that only activates once the account is Qualified.

## How the Drawdown Works

Alpha Futures calls its drawdown the Maximum Loss Limit (MLL). It is an End-of-Day (EOD) trailing drawdown applied identically on the Evaluation and Qualified stages of every Alpha Futures account: "The Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is EOD (end of day) trailing on all of our accounts here at Alpha Futures. None of our accounts utilize intraday trailing drawdown you will see elsewhere in industry." The floor is calculated from the account's balance high at the end of each trading day, not intraday equity, and it rises whenever a new EOD high is set; it never moves down on a losing day.

Once the trailing floor would reach the account's own starting balance, it permanently stops trailing there: "Maximum Loss Limit stops trailing at the account starting balance on all of our accounts... Once the Maximum Loss Limit reaches the initial starting balance, it won’t continue to trail." For Standard 50K, starting at $50,000 with a $2,000 MLL, the floor starts at $48,000 and locks permanently at $50,000 once EOD balance reaches $52,000 (see the Drawdown Lock row above for how this figure is derived).

Breaching the MLL at any point, whether floating intraday equity or the EOD closed balance, liquidates the account immediately: "If you break MLL at any point, floating equity or closed balance of account, your account will be liquidated." On an Evaluation this means the account can no longer become a Qualified Account (a Reset or a fresh Evaluation is required to try again); on a Qualified Account it means the account is closed.

The Daily Loss Guard is a separate, soft-breach mechanic that only applies once Standard is Qualified; there is none on the Evaluation. At the Evaluation stage the Standard Account Overview states the failure condition is exhaustive: "There is only one way to fail on Evaluation Accounts, do not allow the Account Balance to hit or exceed the Maximum Loss Limit." If it is hit, trading is locked for the rest of the day, resuming the next trading day at 6PM ET, and the account itself is not lost: "If you hit the Daily Loss Guard, you do not lose your Account, but your account will be locked until the next trading day (6PM ET)." Because the MLL and the Daily Loss Guard are independent mechanics, it is possible to hit the MLL floor without first hitting the Daily Loss Guard if the high-water mark is close enough; in that case the account fails on the MLL breach regardless of the Daily Loss Guard.

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (for example, a $50,000 account showing $1,500 profit reads as a $51,500 balance), matching the MLL article's own convention, and never switches to a $0-based profit figure.

**Evaluation:**

1. Trader starts a $50K Standard Evaluation at $129/month, rebilling monthly until passed or cancelled. The account opens at a $50,000 balance, a $3,000 profit target, a $2,000 MLL (EOD trailing, no Daily Loss Guard during Evaluation), a 50% consistency rule, and a 2-day trading-day minimum. The floor starts at $50,000 minus $2,000, or $48,000.
2. EOD Day 1: the balance rises to $51,500, a new EOD high and a $1,500 net profit for the day. The floor trails up to $51,500 minus $2,000, or $49,500, still below the $50,000 starting balance, so it has not locked yet.
3. EOD Day 2: the balance rises to $53,000, a new EOD high and $3,000 cumulative net profit, exactly the profit target. Because EOD balance now exceeds $52,000 (the account's $50,000 starting balance plus its $2,000 MLL amount), the MLL has reached the starting balance and stops trailing: it locks permanently at $50,000 rather than the naturally-trailed $51,000 ($53,000 minus $2,000).
4. Consistency check: the largest single day, $1,500 on Day 1, is exactly 50% of the $3,000 total net profit, at, not above, the 50% consistency ceiling. This matches the Consistency Rule article's own recommended figure for a Standard 50K account: "50k | $3,000 | $1,500 or less" (table row). Two minimum trading days are satisfied. The Evaluation is passed.

**Qualified:** sources read for this file do not state whether the Qualified account's opening balance carries forward the Evaluation's ending balance ($53,000 above) or resets to the nominal $50,000 starting balance. This example assumes a fresh $50,000 reset, consistent with the Reset article's own description: "A Reset allows you to return your trading account to the default settings. This includes starting Account Balance, Maximum Loss Limit, and Trading Days." This lets the MLL-lock and Daily Loss Guard mechanics be shown from their own starting points; see Not Confirmed.

5. Trader activates the Standard Qualified account at a fresh $50,000 balance, a $2,000 MLL (floor again at $48,000), a $1,000 Daily Loss Guard (soft breach), and the 40% Qualified consistency rule.
6. Over several sessions the trader accumulates 5 winning trading days, not necessarily consecutive, each with $200 or more profit, reaching a $52,000 EOD balance, a $2,000 net profit. By the same rule shown in step 3, the MLL locks permanently at $50,000.
7. Trader requests a $1,000 performance fee: above the $500 minimum, under the $3,000 per-cycle cap for this size, exactly at the firm-wide "up to 50% of the profit in your account each withdrawal request" cap (50% of the $2,000 cycle profit), and with no single day's profit in the cycle at or above 40% of the $2,000 cycle profit ($800), assumed satisfied for this illustration since no real trade-by-trade data exists to check. The MLL does not slide toward $0 after the request: "If MLL was at $49,200 before payout, it will be after as well." Because this is the account's first virtual payout, the trader receives 70% of the requested $1,000, or $700, under the signed General Service Agreement's tiered schedule (see the Profit Split row above). Balance after the debit: $52,000 minus $1,000, or $51,000, which re-derives from the first figure as $50,000 + $2,000 − $1,000; the locked $50,000 floor is unchanged.
