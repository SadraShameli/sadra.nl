## Overview

Alpha Futures Direct launched July 7, 2026 as a one-time-fee product that skips the Evaluation stage entirely: buyers start trading immediately on a Qualified Account instead of passing a profit-target Evaluation first ("No Evaluation, jump straight into a Qualified Account from day one," Direct Account Overview). Unlike Zero, Standard, and Advanced, Direct Qualified accounts carry no winning-trading-day withdrawal requirement in the sources that describe the Direct payout gate (the Terms' Schedule 2 separately lists "Minimum Virtual Trading Days" 5 on the Direct Qualified row; see Not Confirmed) and no Scaling Plan, and the Reset article's own account-type list ("Qualified Resets are only available on Zero and Standard Accounts") confirms Direct has no Qualified Reset option: a breach simply ends the account, and a new Direct account can be purchased to start again ("What happens if I breach my Direct account? A breach ends that Qualified account," product/direct FAQ). Instead of the 5-winning-day cadence used by Zero, Standard, and Advanced, eligibility for a Direct withdrawal request is gated by reaching a Withdrawal Profit Target (which resets, non-cumulatively, after each successful withdrawal, per "Leftover profit from the previous cycle does not carry over") and a 20% Consistency Rule, both detailed below. Firm-wide trading-conduct rules that carry no plan exception in their own text, including the End of Day Maximum Loss Limit mechanic ("on all of our accounts"), the automation/copy-trading and other prohibited-practice restrictions ("in Evaluation and Qualified stages"), and the CME price-limit rule ("enforced on ALL Alpha Futures Accounts"), apply to Direct Qualified accounts the same as any other Alpha Futures account.

## How the Drawdown Works

Alpha Futures' Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is an End of Day trailing drawdown on every Alpha Futures account, including Direct: it recalculates only at the close of each trading day, trailing up on a new EOD balance high and holding in place on a losing day (it never trails down). Once the trailing floor reaches the account's own starting balance, it stops trailing permanently and locks there for the account's lifespan. The MLL is enforced against floating or closed balance at any point during the day; breaching it at any moment liquidates the account immediately, a permanent failure distinct from the Daily Loss Limit (a same-day soft breach that only pauses trading, without failing the account, until the next session).

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (e.g., a $50,000 account showing $500 profit reads as a $50,500 balance), matching how Alpha Futures' own MLL example presents figures, and never switches to a $0-based convention. The $50,000 opening balance is read from the "50K" tier label (Starting Balance is Unconfirmed), so the floor, lock and balance figures below are derived from it, not quoted; each step marks this.

1. Trader purchases the 50K Direct Qualified account for its one-time fee of $519. There is no Evaluation: the account opens directly as a Qualified Account.
2. The account opens at a $50,000 balance (read from the tier label). Its EOD Trailing MLL is $2,000, so the floor starts at $50,000 − $2,000 = $48,000 (derived). The Daily Loss Limit is $1,000 and the Consistency Rule is 20%.
3. EOD Day 1: the balance falls to $49,500 (a $500 loss). This is not a new EOD balance high, so the floor does not move: a trailing MLL floor only rises on a new high and holds on a losing day. Floor stays at $48,000.
4. EOD Day 2: the balance rises to $52,000 (a new EOD high, $2,000 net profit, exactly equal to the $2,000 drawdown amount). Per Alpha Futures' own MLL rule, once the floor reaches the account's starting balance it stops trailing permanently: the floor locks at $50,000 and does not move again for the account's lifespan (derived: the $52,000 trigger and $50,000 locked value are arithmetic on the unconfirmed Starting Balance).
5. From this point the floor is fixed at $50,000 regardless of any further EOD balance highs. Reaching the $3,000 first Withdrawal Profit Target (profit earned between withdrawal requests, per the Direct Account Overview) and satisfying the 20% Consistency Rule are separate, payout-specific gates, covered in the Payouts section below, not part of the MLL mechanic itself.

Re-derivation check: the example opens at the $50,000 starting balance and the locked floor is derived as (EOD high of $52,000) − ($2,000 drawdown amount) = $50,000, which equals the starting balance stated in step 2, consistent with the source's own "stops trailing at the account starting balance" rule.
