## Overview

Alpha Futures Direct launched July 7, 2026 as a one-time-fee product that skips the Evaluation stage entirely: buyers start trading immediately on a Qualified Account instead of passing a profit-target Evaluation first ("No Evaluation, jump straight into a Qualified Account from day one," Direct Account Overview). Unlike Zero, Standard, and Advanced, Direct Qualified accounts carry no winning-trading-day withdrawal requirement and no Scaling Plan, and the Reset article's own account-type list ("Qualified Resets are only available on Zero and Standard Accounts") confirms Direct has no Qualified Reset option: a breach simply ends the account, and a new Direct account must be purchased to try again ("What happens if I breach my Direct account? A breach ends that Qualified account," product/direct FAQ). Instead of the 5-winning-day cadence used by Zero, Standard, and Advanced, eligibility for a Direct withdrawal request is gated by reaching a Withdrawal Profit Target (which resets, non-cumulatively, after each successful withdrawal, per "Leftover profit from the previous cycle does not carry over") and a 20% Consistency Rule, both detailed below. Firm-wide trading-conduct rules that carry no plan exception in their own text, including the End of Day Maximum Loss Limit mechanic ("on all of our accounts"), the automation/copy-trading and other prohibited-practice restrictions ("in Evaluation and Qualified stages"), and the CME price-limit rule ("enforced on ALL Alpha Futures Accounts"), apply to Direct Qualified accounts the same as any other Alpha Futures account.

## Evaluation

Direct has no Evaluation stage. Per the Direct Account Overview: "Users that select the Direct Account can skip the Evaluation stage and trade in a Qualified Account straight away." The standard Evaluation row set (Starting Balance, Profit Target, Drawdown Type, Drawdown Amount, Minimum Balance at Start, Daily Loss Limit, Max Contracts, Consistency Rule, Minimum Trading Days, News Trading, Inactivity Rule, One-Time Eval Fee, Reset Fee) is genuinely inapplicable to Direct as a category, not merely unconfirmed, so no table is rendered for this section. The one-time account fee that structurally replaces an Evaluation-plus-Activation fee combo for the 50K size is $519 (product/direct; Direct Account Overview's parameters table; how-it-works pricing card all agree). This is a direct entry fee into the Qualified stage described in the ## Sim Funded section below, not an Evaluation fee.

## How the Drawdown Works

Alpha Futures' Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is an End of Day trailing drawdown on every Alpha Futures account, including Direct: it recalculates only at the close of each trading day, trailing up on a new EOD balance high and holding in place on a losing day (it never trails down). Once the trailing floor reaches the account's own starting balance, it stops trailing permanently and locks there for the account's lifespan. The MLL is enforced against floating or closed balance at any point during the day; breaching it at any moment liquidates the account immediately, a permanent failure distinct from the Daily Loss Limit (a same-day soft breach that only pauses trading, without failing the account, until the next session).

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (e.g., a $50,000 account showing $500 profit reads as a $50,500 balance), matching how Alpha Futures' own MLL example presents figures, and never switches to a $0-based convention.

1. Trader purchases the 50K Direct Qualified account for its one-time fee of $519. There is no Evaluation: the account opens directly as a Qualified Account.
2. The account opens at a $50,000 balance. Its EOD Trailing MLL is $2,000, so the floor starts at $50,000 − $2,000 = $48,000. The Daily Loss Limit is $1,000 and the Consistency Rule is 20%.
3. EOD Day 1: the balance falls to $49,500 (a $500 loss). This is not a new EOD balance high, so the floor does not move: a trailing MLL floor only rises on a new high and holds on a losing day. Floor stays at $48,000.
4. EOD Day 2: the balance rises to $52,000 (a new EOD high, $2,000 net profit, exactly equal to the $2,000 drawdown amount). Per Alpha Futures' own MLL rule, once the floor reaches the account's starting balance it stops trailing permanently: the floor locks at $50,000 and does not move again for the account's lifespan.
5. From this point the floor is fixed at $50,000 regardless of any further EOD balance highs. Reaching the $3,000 first Withdrawal Profit Target (a net profit of $3,000 above the $50,000 starting balance, i.e., a $53,000 balance) and satisfying the 20% Consistency Rule are separate, payout-specific gates, covered in the Payouts section below, not part of the MLL mechanic itself.

Re-derivation check: the example opens at the $50,000 starting balance and the locked floor is derived as (EOD high of $52,000) − ($2,000 drawdown amount) = $50,000, which equals the starting balance stated in step 2, consistent with the source's own "stops trailing at the account starting balance" rule.

## Not Confirmed By This Source

- **Starting Balance** — no sentence in any read source literally states "Starting Balance: $50,000." The figure is derived from the plan's own "50K Direct Qualified" tier label, repeated consistently across every table read. Treated as a confirmed table-lookup value per this file's sourcing rules, not a literal quote. Do not assume any source states this figure as a literal dollar amount rather than a size-tier label.
- **Minimum Balance (ongoing)** — not stated in any read source as a concept distinct from the account's nominal starting balance or the MLL floor itself. Do not assume it equals the locked-floor value ($50,000) or the Withdrawal Profit Target balance ($53,000 for the first cycle) without a direct statement.
- **Buffer Requirement** — no read source uses the word "buffer," or describes any balance-floor gate for Direct distinct from the Withdrawal Profit Target (itself already documented as Payout Frequency's gating condition). Do not assume a separate buffer figure exists beyond the Withdrawal Profit Target.
- **Max Payout per Cycle** — contradicted between cited sources for the 50K size. Payout Policy ("50k Direct Qualified AccountMaximum Withdrawal: $2,000"), Direct Account Overview (Withdrawal Limit row, "$500 minimum,$2,000 maximum" under Direct 50K) and product/direct ("Payout Cap" / "$2,000" on the 50K Direct Qualified card) all state $2,000, but Maximum Withdrawal Request (same article-stated update date, 2026-07-27) states "50k Direct Qualified Account maximum withdrawal is $1,500". The table keeps $2,000 as the figure carried by three sources including the plan's own dedicated overview article; the $1,500 figure is not resolved by any source read this pass. Do not assume either figure is current without checking the live Payout Policy at request time.
- **Maximum Total Payouts / Lifetime Cap** — not stated in any read source, and unlike Growth's open-ended payout-tier table, Direct's Max Payout per Cycle is a flat, non-escalating figure with no "+"-style notation to infer from. Do not assume there is no cap, and do not assume one exists.
- **"Up to 50% of profit per request" cap, cross-plan-carryover risk, flagged not applied** — the Payout Policy article's own "core similarities" preamble scopes this rule explicitly to "both Evaluation to Qualified account types (Standard, Advanced, and Zero)," and the dedicated Performance Fee Rules Explained post scopes its "Core performance fee rules (all plans)" table explicitly to "Zero, Advanced, and Premium." Direct is never listed in either article's plan breakdown. A separate line on how-it-works.txt's FAQ ("You may withdraw up to 50% of your profit per request") appears in a paragraph that also discusses Direct, without explicitly excluding or including Direct, creating an unresolved conflict between sources. This file does not apply the 50%-of-profit cap to Direct; only the fixed, Direct-specific Max Payout per Cycle values above are used. Do not assume Direct's payout size is capped at 50% of account profit.
- **Payout processing time** — two firm-wide sources conflict and neither is written into a table row (the template has none for this): the Performance Fee Rules post states "processing is 48 business hours or less" (itself scoped away from Direct per the item above), while the more recent Payout Update post states "the current timeline is five business days or less" for "a standard Alpha Futures payout" without naming plans. Do not assume either figure applies to Direct without checking current policy at request time.
- **Live/Alpha Prime transition trigger for Direct specifically** — how-it-works.txt's FAQ states the +$40,000-payable-balance / 5-payout-cycle trigger generically for "your simulated Qualified Account," never naming Direct by name. Direct Qualified accounts are a type of Qualified Account, so this is not a clear case of copying another plan's stated rule, but it is also not a Direct-named confirmation. Do not assume Direct is either included in or excluded from this transition path without a Direct-named source.
- **Account sizes other than 50K** — this file covers the 50K Direct size only (matching the only size this repo's engine currently models for Alpha Futures' other plans). Sources confirm 25K, 100K, and 150K Direct sizes exist with their own one-time fees, MLL/DLL/contract limits, and Withdrawal Profit Targets (Direct Account Overview's parameter table; product/direct). Not applicable to the 50K figures documented above. Do not apply any figure in this file to the 25K, 100K or 150K Direct sizes.
- **DIRECT35 coupon code** — mentioned only in this repo's own `AlphaFutures.ts` engine notes, sourced from a company blog post that is not one of this file's cited sources. Not independently verified here; do not assume it is currently active or apply it to any figure above.
- **Engine scope gap** — `src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/alphafutures/AlphaFutures.ts` has no model for the Direct plan or the Qualified-from-day-one stage at all: its `plans` array only builds Zero, Standard, and Advanced (`buildZeroPlan`, `buildStandardPlan`, `buildAdvancedPlan`), and there is no `AlphaFuturesVariant.Direct` or `buildDirectPlan`. This is a documentation-ahead-of-engine gap, not a doc/engine mismatch to resolve; the engine was not edited as part of writing this file. Separately, as supporting (not proving) context: the engine's Zero/Standard/Advanced implementations each use `lockThresholdAt(0)` with `atProfit: size.maxDrawdown`, which matches this file's derivation of Direct's own MLL lock mechanic (lock triggers when profit reaches the drawdown amount; locked floor equals the starting balance). That is consistent with, but not a substitute for, Direct having its own cited source for that mechanic (the firm-wide MLL article, cited above). Do not read any Direct figure in this file as engine-validated.
- **Profit Split, flat 90% vs. the signed General Service Agreement’s tiered 70/80/90** — this file’s Payouts table records a flat 90%, backed by the Payout Policy article ("All withdrawal requests are the amount that will be removed from your trading account, trader received 90% of this request.") and by the Direct Account Overview’s "90% profit split on every withdrawal". The General Service Agreement at `https://alpha-futures.com/general-service-agreement`, read in full on 2026-09-20, instead defines "Virtual Performance Fees" as tiered: "First two virtual payouts on the account the User receives a 70% Performance Fee, virtual payouts 3 and 4 80%, virtual payouts 5+ on the account the User receives a 90% Performance Fee." Nothing read this pass reconciles the two, and the Agreement is the document a trader accepts at signup. Do not treat the 90% figure as settled for a trader’s first four payouts on an account.
