## Overview

Take Profit Trader (TPT) documents three account stages: Test (paid monthly evaluation), PRO (sim-funded, real-money payouts on simulated trading) and PRO+ (live, real capital traded directly at the exchange). This file covers PRO+, the last stage TPT's help center describes. "Advantages of PRO+" says that once a trader becomes eligible for an upgrade "an official invitation will be sent via email by the Take Profit Trader team" and that the decision "is made solely at the discretion of Take Profit Trader". Which routes lead into PRO+ is Unconfirmed: the upgrade article describes a PRO account being upgraded, "Resetting A PRO+ Account" and the cooldown article name passing an evaluation or activating a passed test, and TPT's homepage dashboard strings describe going "straight into PRO+" based on recent performance; no source reconciles these (see Not Confirmed). For the Test and PRO stages, see `test-pro.md`.

PRO+ accounts trade TPT's own real capital directly at the exchange rather than a simulated fill environment ("Trader directly routed to the exchange, not on the simulation or ''copy traded''"), carry a 90/10 profit split (up from PRO's 80/20), and, per TPT's own materials, have "No buffer zone requirement for withdrawal" ("Advantages of PRO+"). The drawdown mechanic at this stage is an EOD (End-of-Day) trailing drawdown (see `test-pro.md` for how this compares to Test's and PRO's own mechanics, which are outside this file's own sources): carrying over the exact dollar amount of the trader's own originating PRO account's starting drawdown, applied against a PRO+ account that itself begins at a literal $0 balance. A reduced-parameter, discretionary sub-variant, PRO+ Development, also exists for traders showing higher-risk trading patterns. Both the standard PRO+ upgrade mechanic (including a $5,000 PRO-profit freeze) and PRO+ Development are covered in full below. The $5,000 freeze and the upgrade's own PRO-side bookkeeping are not modeled anywhere in this repository's engine (`src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/tpt/TptLive.ts`); PRO+ Development's own contract limits, soft-breach Daily Loss Limit and smaller EOD drawdown are now modeled there, as of the 2026-09-23 engine fix pass, in `buildTptLiveDevelopmentPlan`: see Not Confirmed By This Source and the engine cross-checks noted throughout.

Several firm-wide policies bear on PRO+, each with the stage scope its own article states: the Universal Trading Policies (stated to apply to "all TakeProfitTrader accounts, including Test, PRO, and PRO+"), the Independent Trade Execution Policy (reached for every stage through UTP #4), the Trade Copier Policy (which states no account-type scope of its own), Restricted Countries (worded platform-wide, naming no stage, so its per-stage scope is Unconfirmed, see Not Confirmed), and CME compliance (UTP #3): and are only summarized here to the extent they bear directly on PRO+'s own trading mechanics (see Account Rules & Trading Conduct, below); their full text belongs in this firm's `README.md`, not repeated per plan file.

## Eligibility & Upgrade Process

_This section replaces the template's `## Evaluation`. No source read states a PRO+ fee, profit target or day-count requirement, and none states that PRO+ has none: how a trader enters PRO+, and whether any entry route is purchasable or a pass/fail test, is Unconfirmed (see Not Confirmed, routes into PRO+). The upgrade process below is the one entry process the sources describe in detail, for a PRO account. The Test-stage fee, profit target and day-count rules belong to `test-pro.md`._

**Becoming eligible.** Advancement to PRO+ is based on "a comprehensive review of trading performance and behavior across multiple objective factors," specifically "consistency, risk management, execution patterns, and overall trading discipline over time." TPT's own language is explicit that "there isn't a single threshold that determines promotion": the team reviews performance holistically. TPT's separate "Sustainable Trading Policy" does not mention PRO+ promotion or eligibility and does not call itself the Upgrade Process review; on its own terms it says traders whose profile shows "using maximum position size on the majority of trades," "repeatedly blowing through maximum loss limits," "relying on simulation-specific fill inefficiencies," or "account churning" "can expect closer review, especially once trading live", and where trading "deliberately exploits the simulated environment or reflects reckless risk management, TPT reserves the right to step in at its sole discretion, with or without prior notice." Whether that policy and the PRO+ upgrade review are one process is not stated by any source (see Not Confirmed). Once a trader is deemed eligible, "an official invitation will be sent via email by the Take Profit Trader team"; the decision itself "is made solely at the discretion of Take Profit Trader."

**Upgrade process.** The upgrade is "fully managed by Take Profit Trader and requires action by both the trader and the brokerage": the trader must complete "any required onboarding steps, agreements, or documentation requested during the transition process." Separately, the Power of Attorney (POA) form is signed earlier, at PRO-account creation, specifically "to ensure that any potential upgrade to a PRO+ account will proceed smoothly and efficiently"; it is "a crucial document required for the opening of a PRO+ account with Tradovate, our designated broker at TakeProfit" (NinjaTrader's logo appears on the form because "NinjaTrader owns Tradovate"). Once all requirements are satisfied, PRO+ setup "typically takes several days," and the trader is notified once it is ready.

As part of the final setup stage, the account is upgraded to professional market-data access, which "can take up to 12 hours, during which trading on both your PRO and test accounts will be temporarily unavailable": the trader is notified in advance of this window. Once the upgrade completes, "all future trading and withdrawals must be done on the PRO+ account only."

**Structure once PRO+ is issued.** Three things happen simultaneously:

1. The originating PRO account is placed on hold.
2. **$5,000 of the PRO account's own profit is frozen** and "must remain in the account while the PRO+ is active." Anything above that $5,000 "can be withdrawn immediately according to the PRO Account Profit Split" (PRO's normal 80/20 split).
3. The PRO+ account itself begins at a literal **$0 balance**, with an initial EOD drawdown "equivalent to the starting drawdown of the original PRO account." TPT's own worked example: "if the PRO account was a $50,000 account, the PRO+ account would start at $0 with an EOD drawdown of -$2,000." As trades become profitable, "the minimum account balance will trail accordingly... stopping once it reaches $0, where it will remain": see How the Drawdown Works, below, for the full mechanic.

**In case of account loss.** If the trader loses the PRO+ account, two things determine what becomes withdrawable:

- "Any remaining profits within the PRO+ account, along with the trader's share of the frozen $5,000 profit from the PRO account, will be eligible for withdrawal" (the source names no percentage for that "trader's share" anywhere in this article; see Not Confirmed).
- "If the PRO+ account ends with a negative balance, that amount will be deducted from the $5,000 profit held in the PRO account. The remaining balance will be available for withdrawal, subject to the standard PRO account profit split terms": i.e., any PRO+ shortfall is deducted from the frozen $5,000 first, and the remainder is then subject to those terms (PRO's own split is 80/20 per "PRO Account Profit Split & Withdrawal Rules"; that this is the split meant by "standard PRO account profit split terms" is not stated, see Not Confirmed).

_Illustration only (round, hypothetical numbers: not a source-stated example): a trader upgrades from a $50,000 PRO account with $5,000 frozen. The PRO+ account is later lost with a −$300 balance. That $300 is deducted from the frozen $5,000 first, leaving $4,700. That $4,700 is then released under the PRO account's standard split, taken here as 80/20 (an assumption, see Not Confirmed), i.e. $3,760 to the trader._

**Not modeled by the engine.** None of the above: the $5,000 freeze, the PRO-account-hold, the $0-start/carried-drawdown mechanic's _origin_, or the account-loss deduction order: is modeled anywhere in `src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/tpt/TptLive.ts`. The engine's `buildTptLivePlan` hard-codes a single $2,000 `DRAWDOWN_AMOUNT` (matching only the $50,000-origin case) with no freeze, no PRO-side accounting, and no loss-handling logic at all.

The Daily Loss Limit is a **soft breach**: it "pauses you, doesn't fail you," unlike a hard DLL that would close the account. Payout frequency and cycle limits are stated directly, not inferred: "The payout policy remains unchanged. Daily payouts with no withdrawal limits." The 90/10 Profit Split is now directly confirmed too, not merely inferred from that "unchanged" framing: the separate "PRO+ Development: 30-Day Live-Capital Cooldown" article states "Live payouts split 90/10, so for example, when a trader withdraws $10,000 from a PRO+ Development account, the trader keeps $9,000 and TPT's $1,000 share reduces that trader's 30-day total by $1,000."

**Who gets moved here.** Placement is discretionary, "based on a review of your overall trading performance, strategy, and risk behavior across both PRO and live accounts," reflecting "patterns over time, not any single trade or blown account." Named patterns: "aggressive sizing," "rapid drawdowns," having "blown through loss limits," "reckless risk management," or "single-session losses that put account longevity at risk": alongside, in different words, the "higher-risk profile" patterns listed in TPT's separate "Sustainable Trading Policy" (max position sizing on most trades, repeatedly blowing through loss limits, account churning); neither article names the other, and no source states that placement in PRO+ Development is that policy's review (see Not Confirmed).

**Path back to standard PRO+.** Placement "isn't a permanent placement." TPT states it wants to see "roughly 60 days of consistent, disciplined risk management... appropriate position sizing, controlled drawdowns, and trading that protects your account": explicitly "not... a specific profit number." A trader may also "request a manual review at any time" by emailing `development@takeprofittrader.com` with their User ID and relevant context, and may keep trading both Test and PRO+ Development accounts while that review is pending.

**The 30-Day Live-Capital Cooldown.** Per "PRO+ Development: 30-Day Live-Capital Cooldown," TPT tracks, over any rolling 30-day window, a trader's net loss of TPT's own live capital across all of that trader's PRO+ Development accounts. If that net loss reaches $10,000, a cooldown is triggered: "new purchases are paused for 30 days," meaning the trader cannot purchase new subscriptions, test resets, or PRO resets, and "cannot receive a PRO+ standard account" for that same period. This is a restriction, not an added privilege, and the article states "This only applies to PRO+ Development accounts," so not to standard PRO+. A cooldown trader is not locked out of trading entirely, though: "They can still trade any active test, PRO, or PRO+ account and can activate passed tests into PRO+ Development accounts": the article lists this among what a trader in cooldown can still do and states nothing further about it, and TPT's homepage dashboard string reads "Due to your active Cooldown status, you are only eligible for a PRO+ Development account" (see Not Confirmed, routes into PRO+). "After 30 days, purchasing can reopen following a standard review from our risk team."

The $10,000 net-loss figure is calculated narrowly, counting only TPT's own capital, not a trader's: only the portion of a loss that falls below an account's own starting balance counts ("Only losses that go below an account's starting balance. That portion is TPT's live capital loss"); profit a trader built and then gave back does not count ("If an account starts at $0, grows to $2,000, and later closes at $0, TPT's capital was never touched. That's $0 toward the cooldown"); withdrawn trader profit does not count ("Trader profit that is withdrawn. Paying a trader is not a loss"); and test/reset/activation fees do not count either direction. TPT's own 10% share of every live payout credits back against the running total ("Live payouts split 90/10, so for example, when a trader withdraws $10,000 from a PRO+ Development account, the trader keeps $9,000 and TPT's $1,000 share reduces that trader's 30-day total by $1,000"). On a breach, the article states the account's actual ending balance is what counts toward the total, not its drawdown/maximum-loss limit ("we use the account's actual ending balance, not its maximum loss limit").

## How the Drawdown Works

PRO+'s EOD (End-of-Day) Drawdown trails on realized profit at the end of the trading day (source: "If the trader earns $500 in realized profit at the end of trading day, the minimum balance would trail $500 up"). Per "PRO+ Account Rules": "Your account balance—including open positions—must not reach or exceed the maximum EOD drawdown limit." The floor starts at the account's $0 starting balance minus the carried drawdown ("-$2,000" in the source's $50,000-origin example) and trails upward: TPT's own description is that "the minimum account balance will trail accordingly. If the trader earns $500 in realized profit at the end of trading day, the minimum balance would trail $500 up, stopping once it reaches $0, where it will remain." The source gives that one dollar-for-dollar example and no cumulative profit level at which the floor reaches $0 (arithmetically, trailing dollar-for-dollar from −$2,000, it would be $2,000 of cumulative profit: derived, not stated). It also does not state when during the day the balance check applies, or what happens on a losing day (see Not Confirmed).

### Worked Example

This example uses a literal $0 starting balance throughout, matching TPT's own statement that "the PRO+ account will begin with a $0 balance." Every number below is the account's own real-dollar balance (never a nominal-plus-profit figure), and each step's floor is computed by hand from the previous step. Dollar-for-dollar trailing is stated only by the source's single $500 example, so steps 3 and 4 apply it as a derived assumption, and steps 5 and 6 flag a reading of Rule 2; each says so. It uses the $50,000-origin PRO+ tier: the only size this file's sources state a concrete EOD Drawdown dollar figure for ($2,000).

1. **PRO+ activation (Day 0):** balance = $0. Per TPT's own worked figure ("the PRO+ account would start at $0 with an EOD drawdown of -$2,000"), the initial floor is $0 − $2,000 = **−$2,000**.
2. **Day 1:** closes $500 in realized profit. EOD balance = $500. Per the source's own $500 example, the floor trails up by the same $500: −$2,000 → **−$1,500**.
3. **Day 2:** closes a further $700 in profit. Cumulative EOD balance = $1,200. Derived (dollar-for-dollar trailing assumed from the single $500 example): floor = −$1,500 + $700 = **−$800**.
4. **Day 3:** closes a further $800 in profit. Cumulative EOD balance = $2,000. Derived: floor = −$800 + $800 = $0, which is where the source says trailing stops ("stopping once it reaches $0, where it will remain"), so the floor **stays at $0** from here on. The $2,000 profit level at which this happens is derived, not stated.
5. **Day 4:** closes a $300 loss. EOD balance falls to $1,700, but the locked floor does not move: it stays at **$0**. PRO+ Account Rule 2 says the account balance, "including open positions," "must not reach or exceed the maximum EOD drawdown limit"; this example reads that limit as the floor, which the source does not state (see Not Confirmed), so on that reading a balance of $0 or below, open positions included, would breach.
6. **Day 5:** closes a further $1,900 loss. EOD balance falls to −$200, at or below the locked $0 floor. On the step 5 reading this breaches the EOD Drawdown. Per the account-loss provision (see Eligibility & Upgrade Process above), "If the PRO+ account ends with a negative balance, that amount will be deducted from the $5,000 profit held in the PRO account": the $200 is deducted, leaving $4,800, which is then "subject to the standard PRO account profit split terms" (no percentage is applied here).

**Payout mechanics.** Withdrawing is a two-step process. Step one, account → wallet: balance is "not updated real time," refreshing only once daily "between 8PM - 9PM EST" (confirmed independently by a second article, "Speed of Payouts Update": "The TPT dashboard reflects updated account balances approximately between 8-9PM EST"). Per the "Speed of Payouts Update" (dated July 17, 2026, updated August 20, 2026), this account-to-wallet step is now automated and takes "5-10 seconds" for "the vast majority of Tradovate PRO account withdrawals": but explicitly **not** for PRO+: "Rithmic PRO, PRO+ withdrawals, and a small number of archived Tradovate PRO accounts remain manual for now": the source does not state a duration for this specific step (the "up to 12 business hours, though usually a lot faster" language in this same source article is stated only for the separate wallet-to-bank step, described next). Payout _eligibility_ itself (not gated behind any buffer or day-count per the Buffer Requirement row above) is unaffected by this speed change: only approval latency is. Step two, wallet → bank/PayPal/Wise: a separate admin-approval step, again "up to 12 business hours" though "usually a lot faster," after which bank processing time depends on method.

**Payout methods.** Three options: Plaid (US bank accounts only, "real-time or through ACH transfer," in some cases "1-2 business days"), PayPal, or Wise ("covers 160 countries"). LLC payouts must use Wise, or PayPal "if your LLC has an established PayPal account" (the source says PayPal account, not specifically a business account). All three methods require the receiving account's identity to match the registered TPT user under AML rules: a Plaid mismatch blocks the connection, and a Wise or PayPal mismatch delays the payout for review or gets it denied. For PRO accounts a trader must be flat (no open positions or working orders) at the moment a withdrawal request is submitted; no source states that requirement for PRO+ withdrawals specifically.

**Withdrawal fee.** Free above $250; a flat $50 fee is charged automatically at or below $250 (from the wallet). Selecting PayPal may add PayPal's own additional fees on top, deducted from the withdrawal amount.

**No Open Positions During Prohibited News (PRO+ Account Rules #6).** Must be flat: no open positions or working orders: one minute before, during, and one minute after: FOMC statements/announcements (Wednesdays, 2:00 PM ET; Fed speakers and FOMC meeting minutes are allowed), Non-Farm Payroll (monthly, Fridays 8:30 AM ET), and CPI. **No Counter Positions (PRO+ Account Rules #4; UTP #5; firm-wide Rule 6).** Traders may not hold opposing positions in the same or closely related product across any accounts under their own control, whether alone or coordinated with another trader. This applies "across all account types: Test, PRO, and PRO+." Named correlated pairs (list explicitly "not exhaustive"): ES↔MES, NQ↔MNQ, YM↔MYM. Hedging using correlated products is also prohibited, not just identical-contract offsetting ("Can hedging strategies using related futures be used? No."). A violation liquidates **both** accounts automatically and forfeits all profit in the violating account, detected by what Rule 6 calls "enhanced compliance-monitoring technology" operating "in near real time"; the "Counter-Positions Report" is the PDF linked in the notification email sent after detection. Traders are fully responsible for violations a trade copier causes, including ones they did not intend (per Rule 6's own FAQ: "Traders are fully responsible for the use of any trade copiers or third-party automation tools... Any trades executed through a copier or automation tool are treated as the trader's own actions under all rules and policies"). Rule 6's own text calls out PRO+ specifically as at stake: enforcing it protects "the firm's ability to offer live-market capital to funded accounts (e.g. PRO+)."

**Avoid the Limit Up/Down (PRO+ Account Rules #5).** Traders must exit all open positions before a CME price limit is reached; current daily price limits are published on the CME website. If a price limit is hit and the position was not exited, the trader "will lose your PRO+ Account."

**Universal Trading Policies (UTP).** Apply firm-wide, across Test, PRO, and PRO+: #1 no trading bots, algos, or automated execution tools at all (fully prohibited, not conditionally permitted); #2 do not exceed the maximum position size for the account type; #3 comply with CME Group requirements; #4 the Independent Trading Requirement (below); #5 no counter-position trading (above); #6 trade approved products during approved times only, no overnight holding. Violating any UTP "may result in profit forfeiture, account reset, account closure, or removal from the platform."

**Independent Trade Execution Policy & Trade Copier Policy.** Trade copiers and similar execution/order-management tools are explicitly permitted, but only across accounts a trader personally owns and controls, and only when they do not create coordinated, opposing, or risk-neutralizing positions across those accounts or with other traders. Approved copier tools are named explicitly: Tradesyncer, TradeCopia, Affordable Indicators, Replikanto Flowbot ("Compliance Edition only": standard or modified versions are not permitted), and platform-native copier tools (Tradovate, NinjaTrader, MotiveWave, Quantower, and similar). Traders are fully responsible for violations a copier causes.

**Self-Match Prevention (SMP).** All PRO+ accounts operate under a single, unified SMP ID at the CME Globex exchange level. If a trader's own buy and sell orders would match against each other (or against another PRO+ account sharing TPT's firm-level ID), CME Globex automatically cancels one order before a match occurs: this enforces CME Rule 534 (Wash Trades Prohibited). TPT describes the resulting occasional "rejected by CME" message as normal, rare, and not damaging to account standing; the order can simply be resubmitted.
