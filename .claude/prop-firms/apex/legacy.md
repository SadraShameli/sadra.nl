## Overview

Legacy is Apex's original Evaluation-to-Performance-Account product line. Apex's own Legacy Products Overview article states plainly that "Apex Trader Funding has retired the previous versions of the Evaluation and Performance accounts. As of March 1st, 2026, these prior versions are no longer available for purchase," and separately, "as of March 1st, 2026, Legacy evaluation accounts are no longer available for purchase to anyone." This directly conflicts with the live Legacy Accounts product page (fetched the same day, 2026-09-19), which actively advertises "Legacy Accounts – Available Now – Limited Time" with working checkout pricing across all three platforms and six account sizes, framed as "Limited-Time Offer." A third source resolves the shape of the conflict without fully resolving the conflict itself: the (new-product) Inactivity Policy on Performance Accounts (PA) article explicitly carves out its own scope to include "promotional Legacy accounts purchased after March 1st, 2026" as a real, current population distinct from pre-March-1 purchasers. This file documents the plan as a currently-purchasable, promotionally-re-offered Legacy Evaluation-to-PA path, consistent with the live product page and the inactivity article's own self-scoping language, and flags the Legacy Products Overview article's blanket "no longer available to anyone" statement as contradicted, not silently preferred over the other. See Not Confirmed.

Unlike Apex's current-generation Intraday and EOD products (one-time 30-day-access Evaluation fee, no reset mechanism, fixed 7-calendar-day PA activation window), Legacy retains its original mechanics: the Evaluation is a recurring MONTHLY subscription (not a one-time fee), Evaluation resets are available for a fee (PAs cannot be reset), and PA activation is a one-time Lifetime fee with no fixed expiration deadline of its own -- the passed Evaluation subscription simply keeps billing monthly until the trader either pays the PA fee or cancels. Legacy's Trailing Drawdown follows the account's peak balance (live and unrealized), stops trailing once specific thresholds are hit, and those lock thresholds differ by trading platform during the Evaluation stage only (Rithmic, Tradovate, WealthCharts): see How the Drawdown Works. Legacy carries no Daily Loss Limit at any stage, a 30% (not 50%) payout-consistency rule, and a two-tier (half-then-full) contract-scaling rule on the PA stage rather than the current products' profit-banded tier tables (whose tier count varies by account size).

This file documents the "50K FULL" size on any of the three platforms (Rithmic, Tradovate, WealthCharts identically priced), the only size and account type ("Full," as opposed to the separate "Static" drawdown variant available only at 100K) with a live-source price and rules citation set gathered for this file. Sourced figures for Legacy's other five sizes (25K, 100K, 150K, 250K, 300K) and for the 100K Static variant are given as context only in Not Confirmed, not modeled in the tables below. **The engine at `src/lib/prop-calculator/firms/apex/` (`ApexTraderFunding.ts`, `ApexLive.ts`) has no Legacy model at all** -- neither plan definition nor drawdown/payout logic exists for Legacy in this codebase; see Not Confirmed.

## How the Drawdown Works

The Legacy Trailing Drawdown follows the account's peak balance, including unrealized (open, live) profit, and only ever moves up, never down: "the trailing threshold is based on the highest live value during trades, not on closed trade values." The initial threshold sits Drawdown-Amount below the starting balance ($47,500 for this 50K plan, $50,000 − $2,500). If the balance falls below the threshold at any point, the account fails (Evaluation) or is liquidated (PA) immediately, regardless of whether the position later recovers.

The point at which the threshold stops trailing and locks differs by stage, and during the Evaluation stage only, by trading platform:

- **Evaluation, Rithmic:** "the drawdown stops trailing when the threshold balance reaches your profit target (not the account balance). For instance, a 50K account stops trailing when the threshold reaches $53,000." Locked value: $53,000 (the Profit Target Balance, Starting Balance + Profit Target). Because the threshold trails Drawdown-Amount below the peak, this locked value is first reached once the peak balance hits $53,000 + $2,500 = $55,500 -- this $55,500 trigger figure is this file's own derivation from the source's stated trailing formula, not a number stated verbatim anywhere in the source; see Not Confirmed.
- **Evaluation, Tradovate:** "The trailing drawdown continues to trail in evaluations and does not stop." No lock exists at any point during the Evaluation.
- **Evaluation, WealthCharts:** Unconfirmed. The source's "Special Rules for RITHMIC and TRADOVATE Accounts" section states a rule for those two platforms only and is silent on WealthCharts; do not assume WealthCharts follows either the Rithmic or the Tradovate rule. See Not Confirmed.
- **Performance Account (all platforms):** Trigger: peak balance reaches the Safety Net, $52,600 (Starting Balance + Max Drawdown + $100). Locked value: threshold fixes permanently at $50,100 (Starting Balance + $100). Stated without a platform qualifier.

## Legacy-Specific Trading Rules

These named rules are referenced in passing elsewhere in this tree (README.md, this file's own Overview and Sources footer) but their actual mechanics were not previously stated anywhere in the tree. All are cited by at least one dedicated Legacy-scoped article, most by two or more corroborating articles.

**5:1 Risk-Reward Ratio Rule.** "For every trade you make, your stop loss should not exceed five times the amount of your profit target. For example: If your profit target is 10 ticks, your maximum stop loss should be 50 ticks (5 times your profit target). If you set a stop loss beyond 50 ticks, such as 100 ticks, this would violate the rule." Per the dedicated "Legacy 5:1 Risk-Reward Ratio Rule" article, corroborated identically by PA Compliance, the Consistency Rules FAQ, and PA Trading Rules.

**30% Negative P&L (MAE) Rule.** A per-trade risk control, distinct from a Daily Loss Limit (Legacy has none): "the live, unrealized, open negative P&L cannot exceed 30% of the account's profit balance at the start of the day." Example: "For a $50,000 account, if the profit balance is $4,000, a trader should not allow a drawdown exceeding $1,200." **Adjustment Based on Growth:** "If the end-of-day (EOD) profit balance doubles the safety net, traders may use a 50% drawdown limit instead of 30% starting with the next full trading session." Per "Legacy 30% Negative P&L Rule (MAE)," corroborated by PA Compliance.

**Hedging & Correlated Instruments Rule.** "All accounts must be traded directionally. You may not hold opposing long and short positions at the same time in correlated instruments, contract sizes, or across multiple accounts. Hedging or offsetting positions across correlated instruments is not permitted." Consequence: "account closure." Per the dedicated "Hedging and Correlated Instruments Rule" article, corroborated by PA Trading Rules' near-identical wording ("Holding both long and short positions simultaneously on the same or correlated instrument is strictly prohibited... rather than speculative attempts to hedge both sides during a news-driven breakout").

**One-Direction Rule (Directionally Biased Trading), broader than a news-event-only restriction.** "Traders are only allowed to hold a position in one direction at any time—either long (buy) or short (sell). Traders are also prohibited from using a non-directionally biased strategy where they have open orders for both sides of the market." This extends across instruments and accounts: "Traders shall not trade one direction on minis and another direction on micros at the same time. Traders shall not spread trade indices, i.e., long ES, and short YM. All Apex accounts must be traded directionally only and never be both long and short concurrently in the same account or in other accounts in any correlated markets." Per the dedicated "One-Direction Rule" article, corroborated by PA Trading Rules and the Consistency Rules FAQ. This is a stricter, correlated-market-spanning restriction than the News Trading row's "a position may be held in only one direction per event" might suggest on its own; the restriction is not limited to news events.

**Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA) is permitted, without restriction on entry size or timing.** "DCA is allowed under this agreement with no restrictions on contract size for additional entries," and "there are no specific rules for determining entry points, timeframes, or distances from the original order." It remains subject to the consistency and MAE rules above: "DCA is acceptable as long as it does not violate other scaling and consistency rules, such as the 30% profit in a day rule or the 30% negative P&L rule." Per PA Compliance.

**Scaling Rule violation consequences.** A single, accidental violation (more than half the max allowed contracts traded): the trader must close the excess immediately; profits generated from the violation are removed; and the account is reset "to the EOD balance from the previous trading day, which is the day before the first scaling rule violation occurred," with 8 additional compliant trading days required before becoming eligible for another payout. Blatant or repeated violations: "account closure and forfeiture of all balances." Per the Consistency Rules FAQ (fuller wording, including the EOD-balance-reset consequence); PA Compliance corroborates with slightly abbreviated wording that omits the reset detail.

**Manipulation or exploitation of the simulated trading environment, including High Frequency Trading (HFT), is strictly prohibited.** Per "Legacy PA Payout Parameters."

**Account and resource sharing is prohibited.** "Sharing MAC addresses, computers, IPs, credit cards, or trade copying with other traders is strictly forbidden. Violations will result in account closure, forfeiture of funds, and potential additional verification or audits to ensure compliance." Per "Legacy PA Payout Parameters." A comparable enumeration (MAC address, computer, IP, credit card, trade copying) appears in `live.md`'s own "Other prohibited activities" section; do not assume the two are identically scoped without checking `live.md`'s own citation.

### Worked Example

This example tracks the account's full nominal balance throughout (a $50,000 account showing $500 profit reads as a $50,500 balance), not a $0-based profit figure, and never switches to a $0-based convention.

**Evaluation (Rithmic):**

1. Trader begins a $50K Legacy Evaluation (a recurring $197/month subscription, any platform). The account opens at a $50,000 balance, a $3,000 profit target, no Daily Loss Limit, and a $2,500 Trailing Drawdown -- initial threshold: $50,000 − $2,500 = $47,500.
2. First trade: unrealized profit lifts the balance to $50,500. The threshold updates to $50,500 − $2,500 = $48,000, per the source's own identical worked example.
3. Second trade: the balance peaks intraday at $50,875 but the trader closes the trade at $50,100. The threshold trails to the peak, not the closed value: $50,875 − $2,500 = $48,375, "even though you closed at a lower amount."
4. Trading continues across further sessions. Because the Rithmic evaluation-lock trigger derived above is $55,500 (peak balance), and the account never reaches that peak before the profit target is met, the threshold keeps trailing normally for the rest of this example.
5. After accumulating 7 non-consecutive trading days, the balance closes at $53,000 (Starting Balance $50,000 + the $3,000 profit target) without ever breaching the trailing threshold. The Evaluation passes.

**Performance Account (PA):** sources read for this file do not state whether the PA's opening balance carries forward the Evaluation's ending balance ($53,000 in this example) or resets to the nominal $50,000 starting balance. This example assumes a fresh $50,000 reset for illustration; see Not Confirmed.

6. The Evaluation subscription keeps billing monthly until the trader pays the one-time $140 Legacy PA Lifetime Fee for this 50K size (the only activation-fee option for a Legacy account purchased on or after March 1, 2026); paying it automatically cancels the Evaluation subscription. The PA opens at a fresh $50,000 balance, a $2,500 Trailing Drawdown (threshold again at $47,500), no Daily Loss Limit, and an initial half-contract limit of 5 Minis (of the 10-Mini max).
7. Trading continues and the peak (live/unrealized) balance reaches $52,600 (Starting Balance + Max Drawdown + $100, the Safety Net). This is the exact PA drawdown-lock trigger: the threshold, previously trailing at (peak − $2,500), stops moving and locks permanently at $52,600 − $2,500 = $50,100.
8. Separately, once the account's End-of-Day (closed) balance also exceeds $52,600, the trader may use the full 10-Mini contract limit starting with the next full trading session (not immediately on the intraday touch from step 7 above -- the contract-scaling trigger is EOD balance, a different condition from the drawdown-lock's peak/unrealized-balance trigger).
9. The account accumulates 8 trading days since PA inception, including 5 of those days each showing $50 or more in net profit, and the balance reaches $52,600, the Minimum Required Balance to request a payout for this size (the same $52,600 figure as the Safety Net trigger above, by design, not a coincidence of three unrelated numbers).
10. Trader requests the minimum payout of $500 (fully within the 100%-of-first-$25,000 band). New balance: $52,600 − $500 = $52,100 -- this "encroaches on the safety net by the $500 allowed," explicitly stated by the source as acceptable for the first three payouts.

## Live Transition

Legacy PAs are Simulated (SIM) accounts that use live market data ("Legacy PAs begin as simulated (SIM) accounts, but they use live market data to replicate real trading conditions"). Per the (near-duplicate) Consistency Rules article, once a trader demonstrates consistent SIM performance, trades are mirrored into a live account via an API, and after "long-term consistency is confirmed, the trader will be contacted regarding a transition to a live account." This transition is explicitly invitation-only, with no numeric trigger (payout count, profit amount, or time period) stated in any source read for this file: "Traders are moved to Live Prop Accounts by invitation only. The Help Desk has no further information regarding hypothetical questions about Live Accounts."

This is a distinct mechanism from the current-generation Apex Live Prop Trading Program documented in [`apex/live.md`](live.md): that file's own Overview explicitly states it "does not use any figure from Apex's discontinued Legacy Evaluation/Performance Account products, which are governed by their own, separate, discontinued-product rules". Do not treat `live.md`'s starting balance, drawdown, level structure, or payout-split figures as describing Legacy's live transition; no source read for this file gives Legacy's own live-account operating parameters beyond "invitation only, trades mirrored via API." See Not Confirmed.

## Billing, Cancellation, and Reset Mechanics

Legacy's recurring-subscription billing model (distinct from the current products' one-time fee) carries its own set of cancellation, grace-period, and reset mechanics, previously referenced only in passing (or not at all) elsewhere in this file.

**Cancellation deadline.** "Cancel before market close on the day prior to your renewal date to stop the next billing cycle." Missing it: "Failure to cancel will result in an automatic charge, and no refunds will be issued."

**A failed Evaluation subscription does not auto-cancel; a failed Performance Account subscription does.** "No, failing an evaluation does not automatically cancel your account," corroborated by "Failed Evaluation Account Subscriptions: These accounts do not automatically cancel upon failure. If you no longer wish to continue, you must manually cancel the subscription to avoid further charges." This is the opposite of PA behavior: "Performance Accounts: If your Performance Account fails, the account subscription is automatically canceled. You will need to create a new subscription to continue."

**A failed, non-subscribed Evaluation is disabled after 8 days.** "Accounts that fail and aren't reset within eight days will be disabled if there are no active subscriptions. Reset the account or open a new one to continue."

**A negative balance on renewal is not itself a failure; only a negative drawdown is.** "A negative initial balance upon renewal doesn't mean a failed evaluation; only a negative drawdown will constitute a failure. Accounts in a Failed or Blown state will receive a reset upon renewal."

**A free reset auto-applies on renewal if a failed Evaluation's subscription was left running, subject to a same-cycle deadline.** "If your Evaluation Account fails and you forget to cancel your subscription, a free reset will be applied automatically upon renewal as part of the renewal fee, provided certain conditions are met." The condition: "the Evaluation Account must fail before 5 PM ET on the trading day before the invoice renewal date. Otherwise, the account will not be reset, and balances will remain unchanged."

**A reset never changes the renewal/billing date.** "Resets do not extend your renewal or expiration date. Your renewal date remains exactly 30 days from the last billing date, regardless of resets."

**A separate 48-hour refund window governs the evaluation renewal fee specifically, not the free-reset eligibility above.** "If you pass your Evaluation Account before 5 PM ET on the day before your rebilling date and fully activate your PA account within 48 hours, you are eligible for a refund of the evaluation renewal fee (NOT the original account fee)." Missing it: "If the Evaluation Account passed more than 1 day before your rebilling date, you will not be eligible for refunds of the renewal fee if you have not converted to a PA before the fee is charged." Both this refund window and the free-reset condition above use a "5 PM ET the day before rebilling/renewal" cutoff, but they govern different things (a fee refund vs. a free balance reset) and should not be conflated.

**Failed renewal payments get a 72-hour grace period; missing it terminates the subscription and forfeits profits and passed evaluations.** "we offer a 72-hour grace period for any failed renewal payments... If the subscription is not renewed after the grace period, it will be terminated, including all profits and passed evaluations." Corroborated by an identical 72-hour grace-period statement in Legacy Evaluation Subscription & Billing, and echoed informally by a general troubleshooting article's "settle the payment within 72 hours before the account expires and is no longer salvageable" (a lower-confidence, non-Legacy-specific source, but consistent with the dedicated 72-hour figure).
