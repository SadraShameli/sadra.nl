## Overview

The Apex Live Prop Trading Program is an invitation-based progression from Apex's simulated trading environment into a separate live prop trading department with its own onboarding, documentation, risk oversight, payout process, and trading rules. It is not an Evaluation product and not a Performance Account (PA): a trader cannot hold Live and Sim accounts (Evaluation Accounts or Performance Accounts) at the same time, and moving to Live is entirely at Apex's discretion, based on "trading style, P&L, account count, speed of simulated profit accumulation, and trader history with Apex," not an automatic qualification path a trader can trigger on their own. Once selected, all open Evaluation Accounts close (refunded only if still active at the moment of selection; already-blown accounts are not refunded) and all open Performance Accounts are deactivated, with any positive simulated profit balances tracked in a Bonus Vault (see below) rather than carried over as real trading capital.

The Live account itself is a single, uniform account structure: per the source, "The live account is not based on separate $25K, $50K, $100K, or similar simulated account sizes." It starts at a $0 balance with a $3,000 end-of-day drawdown, scales through four profit-based levels of contract and Daily Loss Limit allowances, and pays out on a flat 90% trader / 10% Apex split. This file covers the Live account's own operating parameters only; it does not cover Evaluation or Performance Account figures (those belong in Apex's own Evaluation/PA plan files), and it does not use any figure from Apex's discontinued Legacy Evaluation/Performance Account products, which are governed by their own, separate, discontinued-product rules (see [legacy.md](legacy.md)).

## How the Drawdown Works

The Live account opens with a $3,000 end-of-day drawdown. The source states only the lock trigger and the locked value for this mechanic, it does not restate a "trailing" formula in Live-specific language (see Not Confirmed for how the engine models the pre-lock behavior). Once the live account's profit balance reaches +$3,100, the drawdown stops and locks permanently at +$100: the account is now safe from closure so long as its balance stays above $100, and it is closed if the balance ever falls to $100 or below. Because this $3,100 lock trigger sits inside Level 1's own $0 to $10,000 profit band, well below Level 2's $10,000 threshold, the drawdown has always already locked by the time an account reaches Level 2 or higher; this is a numeric consequence of the two already-confirmed figures above, not a separately stated fact, and it is the reason no Level 2 to 4 drawdown-amount figure ever appears in the source.

A separate number, the $3,100 "safety net," governs payout eligibility rather than account failure: profit above $3,100 is payout-eligible, profit between $100 (the locked drawdown floor) and $3,100 (the safety net) is not payout-eligible until the account closes. See Payouts below for the full mechanic.

### Worked Example

This example uses a single, literal dollar-balance convention throughout (the account's actual live profit balance, matching the source's own "$0 starting balance"), never switching to a nominal-plus-profit framing.

1. Day 0: a trader is selected for Live and opens a Level 1 account. Starting balance: $0. Contract limit: 10 mini / 100 micro. No Daily Loss Limit. EOD drawdown: $3,000 (pre-lock).
2. Over the following sessions, live profit balance grows to exactly $3,100. This is the exact lock trigger stated in the source ("Once the live account profit balance reaches +$3,100, the end-of-day drawdown locks at +$100"). From this point forward, the drawdown floor is fixed permanently at $100; the account fails only if its EOD balance falls to $100 or below. At $3,100, the trader is still inside Level 1 (below the $10,000 Level 2 threshold), so the 10 mini / 100 micro contract limit and no-DLL status remain unchanged.
3. Balance grows further to $5,000 (still inside Level 1). Per the source, payout requests can be made on profit "above the Safety Net level of $3,100," so the payout-eligible amount is $5,000 minus $3,100, equal to $1,900. This is at least the $500 minimum live payout request amount, so the trader may request up to $1,900.
4. The trader requests the full $1,900 available payout. Per the 90/10 split: trader share is 90% of $1,900, equal to $1,710; Apex retains 10%, equal to $190.
5. After the payout processes, the live profit balance returns to $5,000 minus $1,900, equal to $3,100, the safety net floor. The drawdown floor is unaffected by the payout and remains locked at $100.

Re-derived by hand from the first figure: $0 (start) grows to $3,100 (lock trigger) grows to $5,000, then a $1,900 payout is requested ($5,000 minus $3,100), split $1,710 to the trader and $190 to Apex, leaving a post-payout balance of $5,000 minus $1,900, equal to $3,100. The ending balance ($3,100) matches the balance the account had at the moment the drawdown locked, confirming the arithmetic is internally consistent.

## The Bonus Vault

When a trader moves to Live, any open, profitable Performance Accounts are deactivated and their simulated profit balances are totaled into a tracking ledger called the Bonus Vault. Per the source, it is explicitly "not real money, not real profits, not real gains, not live funds, and not a cash account," with "no rights, obligations, duties, gains, or actual stored value," described as "simply a tracking report." A Performance Account does not need a prior payout to count: "Any open Performance Account with a positive simulated profit balance may be included in the total Bonus Vault amount." Accounts with no simulated profit balance do not contribute.

Eligible traders may receive a monthly Bonus Vault bonus equal to 20% of that month's live withdrawals, deducted from the remaining Bonus Vault balance, sent on the 15th of the following month, and contingent on the live account still being active as of that date. A first-month bonus of 40% (instead of 20%) applies only to the trader's first Live account, for the calendar month containing that account's first approved Live payout.

### Bonus Vault Worked Example (source's own figures)

Quoted and paraphrased directly from the source, which is internally self-consistent:

1. Simulated balances at transition: 5 Performance Accounts x $2,000 each equals $10,000 tracked in the Bonus Vault.
2. Live withdrawal request: trader requests a $10,000 live payout.
3. Live profit split: trader receives $9,000 (90% of $10,000), Apex keeps $1,000 (10%).
4. Bonus Vault bonus: 20% of the $10,000 withdrawal request equals $2,000.
5. Total payout to trader: $9,000 live profit share plus $2,000 Bonus Vault bonus equals $11,000 total.
6. Remaining Bonus Vault: $10,000 minus $2,000 equals $8,000.

Bonus Vault survival across live failures: "During the first three live attempts, 100% of your Bonus Vault balance remains available." After a third live account failure, Apex reviews whether to offer a fourth round; if not offered, "no more simulated products, accounts, services, or payouts of simulated amounts will be available to the trader, including the Bonus Vault." If a fourth round is offered, "only 50% of the remaining Bonus Vault balance will be available for Bonus Vault payouts," with special probation guidelines possibly added. Accounts closed for inactivity are not eligible for Bonus Vault payout bonuses.

## Path to Live (Selection Criteria)

Live selection is not automatic and not something a trader can directly purchase or apply for. Apex "may begin to be monitored for potential live candidacy after one or more of the following occurs," offered as examples, not automatic qualification rules: "Three consecutive withdrawals from one single Simulated PA account," or "Accumulated total simulated profit balances across accounts that indicate the level of trading activity should be generated in the live markets." The source is explicit these are "monitoring guidelines, not automatic qualification rules," and Apex may select a trader earlier than these scenarios or decide more time in simulation is needed.

Once selected, Apex notifies the trader by email and through the members-area notification system, then walks them through live onboarding (documentation, preparation, training, coaching, and CME/live-trading education). A trader may decline the invitation: their Apex user/member account is then fully deactivated, all ongoing Evaluation/PA/simulated services end, and Apex issues "a final reward payment in the amount of $3,000 total, which represents the initial risk amount offered in the live account," processed within 30 days of declining. If declined, "Bonus Vault amounts and any other simulated balances are forfeited."

## Live Account Failure and Requalification

If a live account is lost or blown, the trader may take part in the Second Chance (requalification) process described below to return to live trading; a risk manager may offer suggestions or guidance first, depending on the circumstances of the failure. A trader may only return to the normal simulated Evaluation/PA process "with a special exception from a risk manager," which may cap them at, for example, a maximum of three Performance Accounts, with the first payout from those accounts automatically returning them to live trading.

During the first three live attempts, the full 100% Bonus Vault balance remains available (see The Bonus Vault above). After a third failed live account, a risk manager reviews whether to offer a fourth round; if declined, no further simulated products, services, payouts, or Bonus Vault access remain available; if offered, only 50% of the remaining Bonus Vault balance is available, and special probation guidelines may apply (examples given: smaller starting contract size, contract scaling, Daily Loss Limits, consistency requirements, minimum trading/profit day requirements between payouts, or a required safety net buffer above which withdrawals may be made).

### Longevity Assistance Program

A live risk-support and coaching process, explicitly stated not to be a punishment, intended to help a trader preserve a struggling live account. At any time, including before a fourth live attempt, a live trading coach or risk manager may implement smaller contract size, contract scaling, Daily Loss Limits, consistency requirements, minimum trading day or profit day requirements between payouts, or a required safety net buffer.

## Fees, Platforms, and Trading Rules

**Fees.** Apex does not charge a monthly recurring fee or an up-front one-time fee for the Live account itself. Professional data feeds are "approximately $140 per month," and "Apex covers the expense of the initial main data fee"; additional fees for special or specific instrument data are the trader's own cost and decision. Commissions are set by the broker, not Apex.

**Platforms.** Live trading itself runs on Tradovate or NinjaTrader: "If you use a platform other than Tradovate or NinjaTrader, you must make sure the charting platform is compatible with Ninja Brokerage. You will need a live license key version of the platform to connect to live data and live trading. A temporary, trial, or demo-only platform license will not connect to live trading." **Canadian traders cannot purchase a Live account on Tradovate specifically**: "as a Canadian, you cannot purchase a LIVE account with Tradovate. Instead, you can access their services through Apex accounts," per "Restricted Countries." A Canadian trader selected for Live should not assume Tradovate is available and should default to NinjaTrader instead. This is a different platform lineup from the Evaluation/PA stage's three platforms, Rithmic, Tradovate, and WealthCharts (per "Apex Trader Funding User Accounts": "They may be Rithmic, Tradovate, or WealthCharts accounts," and per "Position Sizing – Evaluation"'s separate per-platform commission/instrument pages). No source read for this file states a platform-dependent variant of the Live account's own drawdown-lock mechanic; the $3,100 trigger / $100 locked floor is stated as one flat figure, with no platform qualifier anywhere in the Live FAQ. See Not Confirmed.

**Core live trading rules,** quoted directly: trade only Apex-approved instruments; "All trades must be closed by 4:50 PM ET. Overnight holding through the market close is not permitted" (this is Live's own, stricter figure than the firm-wide, non-Live-specific "All trades must be closed before 4:59 PM ET" stated in "Futures Trading Times"); "Counter positions, hedging, and group trading are not permitted"; avoid limit-up and limit-down market conditions; follow all CME and applicable exchange rules, the Apex Code of Conduct, and all live prop trading documentation.

**Other prohibitions,** quoted or closely paraphrased: no VPNs, proxies, cloud servers, or location-masking tools used to evade rules, hide violations, or facilitate fraud; every trade must carry a pending or mental stop loss and a defined risk-management strategy; no small-profit-target / large-stop-loss strategies ("a five-tick profit target with a 150-tick stop loss demonstrates unacceptable risk management"); no unsustainable trading or risk-management approaches; strategies and risk management must match "those they would use in a personally funded account at a registered broker"; only the registered account owner may access, verify, or trade the account; no sharing of MAC addresses, computers, IPs, credit cards, or trade copying between traders; no creation of multiple user accounts, including within the same household; no holding open positions through market close; no automation or algorithm usage. A breach of these rules, CME regulations, the Code of Conduct, the Apex User Agreement, or live prop transition paperwork "can result in closure, termination, and forfeiture of the live account and all balances, including simulated balances tracked in the Bonus Vault," and may lead to disciplinary or legal action by the broker, CME, and/or CFTC. "Apex Trader Funding User Accounts" independently confirms that the multiple-user-account prohibition explicitly extends to "live trading environment assessments," not only to simulated accounts.
