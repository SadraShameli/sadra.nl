## Overview

The Legacy Challenge is FundedNext Futures' structured, consistency-rule 1-step futures Challenge, described by the firm as "for traders who prefer structure and risk discipline", offered at $25,000, $50,000, and $100,000. It enforces a 40% consistency rule during the Challenge Phase and carries no Daily Loss Limit at either stage ("The Legacy Challenge has no daily loss limit at any stage", article 17229495; "There is no daily loss limit on the Legacy FundedNext Account", article 17229581), though the Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) still applies throughout. Passing the Challenge moves a trader into the Legacy FundedNext Account, which drops the consistency rule entirely and gates Performance Reward withdrawals behind a Benchmark Day system rather than a fixed payout schedule.

Legacy's live-account stage uses FundedNext's older Reserve/Auto-Liquidation live program, documented separately in [legacy-live.md](./legacy-live.md); see Live Transition below for why none of this file's own figures extend into that file.

## How the Drawdown Works

Legacy's only drawdown mechanic is the Maximum Loss Limit (MLL), and it operates identically whether the account is still in the Challenge Phase or has already become a Legacy FundedNext Account: "The Maximum Loss Limit (MLL) is the total amount you can lose in your Challenge Account or FundedNext Account before the account is permanently breached (a hard breach)." (article 17230292; the older article 14298225 worded it "...before their account is restricted from further trading"). The MLL follows a trailing end-of-day system: it recalculates once per day, at the close of trading, based on the highest balance reached that day ("It updates once per day at the end of the trading day (05:00 PM CT)"). It moves up as the account balance grows, but it never moves back down when the account takes a loss. It only updates at end-of-day, not intraday: "If your floating loss reaches the MLL during a live trade, your account is breached instantly."

Once the trader's profit for the account reaches an amount equal to the plan's own Drawdown Amount ($1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000 at $25K / $50K / $100K), the MLL will have trailed up to exactly equal the account's own initial balance. At that point it locks there permanently and stops trailing further, regardless of how much the balance subsequently grows. If the account balance ever falls to or below this locked floor, the account is hard-breached under the older article 14298225's wording ("If a trader's balance reaches the maximum loss limit, the account is permanently breached."); the current article's $100K example says "If the balance ever falls below $100,000, the account is permanently breached." (article 17230292), so whether a balance exactly at the floor breaches is worded differently by the two (see Not Confirmed).

### Worked Example

This example uses the account's full nominal balance (not a $0-based profit convention), matching the source's own worked example for a $100K Legacy account with an MLL of $3,000 (article 17230292, whose Day 1 to Day 4 figures are the ones below; the older 14298225 opened the same example with "A trader starts with a $100K Legacy Challenge and a maximum loss limit of $3,000. This means the account balance cannot fall below $97,000.")

1. **Start:** Balance = $100,000. MLL = $100,000 − $3,000 = $97,000.
2. **Day 1:** The trader profits $1,000, balance = $101,000. At EOD, the MLL recalculates: $101,000 − $3,000 = $98,000.
3. **Day 2:** The trader profits $2,000 more, balance = $103,000. At EOD, the MLL recalculates: $103,000 − $3,000 = $100,000. Since this equals the account's own initial balance ($100,000), the MLL locks here and will not increase any further.
4. **Day 3:** Balance drops $2,000 to $101,000. The MLL remains locked at $100,000 and does not decrease with the loss.
5. **Day 4:** Balance increases $5,000 to $106,000. The MLL stays locked at $100,000 ("Day 4: Balance rises to $106,000 → MLL stays locked at $100,000.", article 17230292; the older 14298225 added "Despite the increased balance, the maximum loss limit won't increase further beyond the initial balance."). The current article says "If the balance ever falls below $100,000, the account is permanently breached."
